1 · Protocol Prime: objective and subjective projections

22 §1.1 reconstructed Protocol Prime as "enter the projection date as a further control". The one-page Protocol Prime confirms that, and adds a rule the rebuild does not yet apply:

KindWhat the X-Dates areRule
ObjectiveDates not personally tied to the operator: market moves, a team's winsNo extra date required
SubjectiveThe operator's or client's own datesMust end with the date the projection is run, as the last X-Date

The author's reasoning is that the act of measuring becomes part of the measurement, so every subjective projection is unique to the day it is run. The SOP adds that skipping it costs accuracy.

Consequence. natori-on-psyfr has a ☉ Today button that appends the date. Ophis Web has none, and neither build asks whether a cast is objective or subjective.

2 · The filter rules that 22 §6 recorded as undisclosed

22 §6 lists the internals of the Fibonacci, Geodetic Chronometry and IntraDecimal filters as withheld. Other MSRA Filters gives rules for three of them.

Fibonacci Sequence Filter. Set F = {8, 13, 21, 34, 55, 89, 144, 233, 377, 610, 987, 1597}.

  • Operation 1. If Y (X1→X2) is in F, project the members of F forward from X2.
  • Operation 2. Project every member of F forward from X1 and from X2.
  • Either way, a projected date that coincides with a Core Algorithm date marks that date as a Fibonacci Projection. The text says "one of the 15 Core Algorithm dates", which matches the 15-operation v8–v9 table.

This is specific enough to implement, as a tag on Z-Dates, without touching the engine.

Geodetic Chronometry Filter (138). 365.25 × 1.38k gives 504.0, 1008.0, 1512.1, 2016.1 and 2520.2. 365.25 ÷ 1.38k gives 264, 132, 88, 66, 53 and 44. Multiples of 138 run from 138 to 2484.

IntraDecimal Matrix. A number times a smaller decimal copy of itself, for example 819 × 0.819 ≈ 670. The rule is given, but the list of about 50 base numbers is not.

Palindromes. A palindromic count (828, for example) has no value alone. Alongside another match, it adds magnitude.

Use cases: outcomes on scheduled dates; best day to act or wait; when an event recurs; developments in an ongoing matter

Consequence. Casts built from decades-old dates (birthdays, historical events) are outside the author's intended use. The interface could warn about that and group results into windows.

4 · The projection window: 2559 is a diurnal number

22 §4 concluded that the 2559-day default and the author's "2556 day period" were unrelated and only "collided". Diurnal Projections contains both:

  • 365.25 × 7 = 2556.75, the author's "7 years";
  • (365.25 ÷ 3.14) × 22 = 2559.08, the last entry of the same list, matching the 365.25 / 3.14 series (116.32, 232.64 … 2559).

So 2559, which is also the largest Normal MSRF number, is a diurnal value in its own right. The default and the MSRF ceiling share a source; they did not collide by accident. 22 §4 should be amended. The default stays at 2559 for parity either way.

The document also lists the 365.25 × 1.618 and 365.25 ÷ 1.618 series and the 365.25 × 3.14 series, which together make up the Annular Chronometric Filter the MSRF arrays compress.

5 · "On Scaling Vectors": the engine on 26 million graded predictions

A group member's study of the engine on sports results reports:

Level testedResult
Individual MSRF numbers (624)Enrichment ≈ 1.0: no better than the base rate. 504 fires on about 46 % of matches
Density of matchesReal, but explained by match difficulty (EPL) and late-season predictability (NBA), not resonance
MSRF number pairs (162,000+)None survive false-discovery-rate correction
Converging constantsDegenerate: every operation fires on almost every match

Its conclusion is that the engine runs saturated. Any signal is in the composite score, not in which numbers fire. It also reports an MSRF set grown from 628 to 632 by data mining, with 2574, 21978, 6534 and 198 as new members; none of these are in the 390-number arrays shipped here. It also records triangular numbers (2.56×) and Kaprekar numbers (2.08×) as over-represented in the set.

This agrees with this project's own Backtest, which found no edge over chance on the personal test sets. The study's method is worth copying: it applies multiple-comparison correction and compares lift, not raw accuracy.

6 · Context: the prospectus and the rights

Ophis as a Company is marketing. It quotes accuracies (70.5 %, 75.9 %) that no document supplies evidence for, and §5 found no such edge. Don't reuse those figures without a test that supports them.

The SOP states that Protocol Prime "cannot be shared until algorithm bought". The prospectus refers to "Ophis Rights & Privileges" that were not included. Check those terms before publishing the author's methods further, or before any commercial use.

A Z-Date is tagged when an operation's rotation count from its anchor is within 0.1 day of a list value — one display step of rotation_count_z — with a 1e-9 slack so the edge is decided by that rule and not by IEEE-754.

Subjective adds today through the same code as ☉ Today (addTodayAsXDate), in date order, so it is the last X-Date whenever the others are in the past.
